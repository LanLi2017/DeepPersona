# G1 tau-bench success-first calibration v2

Date: 2026-09-17

## Purpose

This is a tau-bench-only calibration for successful strategy equivalence. It
tests whether existing reward-positive trajectories can separate surface and
low-level execution variation from genuinely different solution strategies.
It does not replace the later ALFWorld cross-domain check and is not a final
metric evaluation.

## Data construction

- The complete ALFWorld source has 120 train tasks, 720 trajectories, and 221
  recorded successes. Only two tasks contain three distinct successful action
  sequences; manual inspection found substantial path variation in only one.
  This is insufficient for the proposed success-first cross-domain batch.
- Across four existing tau-bench pools, there are 267 recorded successful
  train episodes. Thirty-three tasks have at least three distinct successful
  tool-name sequences, 29 remain after excluding vacuous targets, and eight
  remain after additionally excluding tool errors and human transfers.
- Audited four existing tau-bench retail rollout pools rather than relying on
  the original single-source seed snapshot.
- Screened 17 additional candidates in three blind quality rounds, then froze
  train task IDs 16, 28, 69, and 75. All have non-vacuous expected database
  writes.
- Required every source trajectory to have recorded reward 1, no missing
  messages, exact parsing of every declared tool call, no tool error, and no
  transfer to a human agent.
- Selected three independently generated, majority-quality-approved successful
  trajectories per task. The deterministic medoid is the anchor; the other two
  remain unlabeled natural candidates. Some trajectories intentionally share a
  tool skeleton, since distinguishing those from a mere surface rewrite is part
  of the calibration.
- Added one mechanically reproducible E0 identifier/serialization transform per
  anchor. Candidate slots are blind and balanced.
- Excluded task 57: its empty action and NL-assertion targets make the DB reward
  vacuous for trajectories that leave state unchanged.
- The screens rejected 7/17 candidates by majority. Failure modes included
  incomplete order coverage, missing confirmation, unauthenticated account
  access, wrong variant claims, and invented balances or delivery timelines.

## Interface

`annotation/tau_success_calibration.html` defaults to an aligned tool-call diff.
Common calls are visually aligned, additions/deletions are highlighted, and
the long conversation is collapsed. Every rubric question now includes an
operational definition. The 12 comparisons are all successful; outcome alone
cannot answer the equivalence question.

## Independent LLM pre-annotation

Three judges independently received only `judge_packets.jsonl`, never hidden
provenance or another judge's output:

- GPT-5.6 Codex (`gpt-5.6-sol`);
- Sonnet (`aws-claude-4-6-sonnet-fair`);
- Opus CLI, which resolved to `claude-opus-5`.

The machine-readable aggregate is generated by
`scripts/summarize_tau_success_judges.py`. LLM consensus is weak supervision,
not ground truth, and must remain hidden until human annotation is complete.

## Result

Run `python scripts/summarize_tau_success_judges.py` to reproduce the exact
agreement and quality table in
`data/g1/tau_success_v2/judgments/summary.json`.

All four retained anchors and all eight retained natural candidates received a
majority high-quality vote in the final panel. Natural candidates received
19/24 individual high-quality votes; anchor votes were 9 yes, 2 no, and 1
unclear. These disagreements remain queued for human review rather than being
silently converted to gold labels.

Equivalence labels were unanimous on 10/12 comparisons, with Fleiss' kappa
0.788 and pairwise agreement from 0.833 to 0.917. All four E0 variants were
recognized by all three judges (12/12 votes). Natural-candidate majority labels
were seven E1 and one E0; none was E2.

## Interpretation and next gate

The LLM-vetted candidate batch is now frozen and ready for independent human
calibration, but it is not human gold. The clean E0 control and strong E1
agreement validate the annotation machinery. The absence of any majority-E2
natural pair is also the central stage result: existing successful rollouts
vary mostly in phrasing and low-level execution, not solution strategy.

Next, collect targeted successful rollouts designed to vary information
coverage, independent-subgoal ordering, and recovery policy. This should use
LLMs to execute distinct plans in the benchmark, not to paraphrase completed
transcripts. Every generated run must pass environment reward, the mechanical
gate, and the same multi-judge quality screen before E1/E2 adjudication.
ALFWorld requires the same targeted collection before any cross-domain claim.
